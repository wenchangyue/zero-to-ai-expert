# Editorial and source policy

Zero to AI Expert explains technical ideas for beginners without hiding the mechanism. Each lesson separates four things: what enters a system, what transforms it, what comes out, and how someone checks the result.

## Source order

Technical claims are checked against official documentation, standards bodies, original research papers, or established textbooks. A company blog may explain how that company's product works, but it does not replace a primary paper or standard for a general claim.

## Transcript labels

A page is called a **reviewed transcript** only when its wording matches the final narration and caption track. A **reviewed lesson companion** may reorganize the same ideas for reading, remove repeated spoken phrases, and add source links. It is not labeled as a transcript.

## Models and complete systems

The site distinguishes a model from the product around it. Interfaces, retrieval, tools, safety rules, logging, and human approval can change what a user receives even when the underlying model stays the same.

## Generated and verified output

Fluent output is not treated as verified evidence. Verification may require a primary source, a calculation, executable code, a test set, or review by a person with the relevant expertise.

## Corrections

Pages record a modified date. When a source changes or an explanation is corrected, the public page and its machine-readable metadata are updated together.
