BIO 101 - LECTURE 7 NOTES: PHOTOSYNTHESIS (fictional course, for a lesson)

What it is
- Plants, algae and some bacteria turn light energy into chemical energy stored in sugar.
- Word equation: carbon dioxide + water --(light)--> glucose + oxygen
- Symbol equation: 6CO2 + 6H2O -> C6H12O6 + 6O2
- Prof. Reyes: on the exam, write the WORD equation. The symbol equation alone gets half marks.

Where it happens
- In chloroplasts.
- Light reactions: thylakoid membranes.
- Calvin cycle: stroma (the fluid around the thylakoids).

Light reactions
- Chlorophyll absorbs mostly red and blue light and reflects green, which is why leaves look green.
- Water is split. The oxygen that plants release comes from WATER, not from carbon dioxide.
- Products passed on to the Calvin cycle: ATP and NADPH.

Calvin cycle
- Uses ATP and NADPH to fix carbon dioxide into sugar.
- The enzyme that grabs CO2 is RuBisCO.
- The direct product is G3P, which the plant uses to build glucose.
- Called "light-independent", but it normally runs in daylight because it needs ATP and NADPH from the light reactions.

Limiting factors
- Light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration, temperature.
- Whichever one is in shortest supply limits the rate.

Exam scope
- The exam covers the light reactions and the Calvin cycle only.
- C4 and CAM plants are NOT on this exam.
